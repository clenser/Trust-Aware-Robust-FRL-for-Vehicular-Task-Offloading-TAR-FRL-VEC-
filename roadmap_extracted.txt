1 Publication-First Project Philosophy
The primary objective of this project is not merely to develop a working software prototype.
The primary objective is to develop a technically defensible research contribution that can be
converted into a research manuscript for submission to a suitable international conference or
journal.
The software implementation will act as the experimental platform for validating the pro-
posed research methodology.
The project should therefore follow the sequence:
Literature→Research Gap→Research Question→Novel Method→
Mathematical Model→Implementation→Experiments→Evidence→Paper
Students should not begin the major implementation before identifying the precise research
gap and defining the proposed contribution.
1.1 Important Principle
The project should not claim novelty merely because it combines:
V ANET + Trust + Federated Learning + Reinforcement Learning.
These are already active research areas.
The novelty must arise from a clearly defined mechanism, algorithm, mathematical formu-
lation, or experimental contribution.
2 Research Problem
In vehicular ad hoc networks (V ANETs), vehicles frequently need to offload computation-
intensive tasks such as cooperative perception, sensor fusion, object detection, and intelligent
route planning to nearby vehicles or edge servers.
However, the highly dynamic nature of vehicular environments creates several challenges:
2

=== PAGE 3 ===
•High vehicle mobility.
•Intermittent connectivity.
•Heterogeneous computational resources.
•Dynamic task arrival.
•Limited communication bandwidth.
•Unreliable participating vehicles.
•Malicious participants.
•Model poisoning attacks.
•Free-riding behaviour.
•Non-IID local training data.
Traditional reinforcement learning based task-offloading methods may assume reliable par-
ticipants and relatively stable environments. Similarly, conventional federated learning ap-
proaches may not adequately distinguish between honest, unreliable, temporarily disconnected,
and malicious participants.
Therefore, this project investigates a trust-aware federated reinforcement learning framework
for secure and efficient task offloading in dynamic vehicular edge environments.
3 Proposed Research Direction
The project will investigate a framework consisting of:
Dynamic Behavioural Trust + Model-Update Trust + Federated Reinforcement Learning
+Robust Aggregation
The proposed framework should evaluate the reliability of a participant using both its his-
torical behaviour and the quality of its federated model update.
A possible high-level trust formulation is:
T f inali (t) =αT behaviouri (t) + (1−α)T modeli (t)
where:
•T behaviouri represents behavioural trust.
•T modeli represents model-update trust.
•αcontrols the relative contribution.
The final mechanism must be refined after the literature and novelty analysis.
4 Research Questions
The students should formally investigate the following research questions.
RQ1: Dynamic Trust
How can participant trust be dynamically estimated when vehicle behaviour, connectivity, and
computational resources continuously change?
RQ2: Behavioural and Model Trust
Can behavioural trust and federated model-update quality jointly identify unreliable and mali-
cious participants more effectively than behavioural trust alone?
3

=== PAGE 4 ===
RQ3: Task Offloading
Does incorporating trust into the reinforcement learning based offloading decision improve task
latency, reliability, and task completion?
RQ4: Federated Robustness
How does the proposed trust-aware federated reinforcement learning framework behave as the
proportion of malicious participants increases?
RQ5: Dynamic Environment
Can the proposed method remain effective under high mobility, intermittent connectivity, het-
erogeneous resources, and non-IID local task distributions?
5 Research Hypotheses
The following hypotheses will guide the experimental evaluation.
•H1:Trust-aware federated reinforcement learning reduces average task latency compared
with non-trust-aware federated reinforcement learning under comparable network condi-
tions.
•H2:Trust-aware aggregation provides higher task reliability and model robustness than
conventional FedAvg under increasing malicious participation.
•H3:Combining behavioural trust with model-update trust provides greater robustness
against model poisoning than behavioural trust alone.
•H4:The proposed framework maintains its performance advantage under different mo-
bility and connectivity conditions.
•H5:The security improvement can be achieved without introducing impractical compu-
tational and communication overhead.
The hypotheses must be evaluated experimentally rather than assumed to be true.
